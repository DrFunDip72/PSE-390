## Summary

The lecture uses Nikola Electric Vehicles and Elizabeth Holmes' cases to illustrate ethical risks in startup marketing, emphasizing the consequences of misleading investors, the importance of transparent communication, and the need for ethical business practices to avoid legal penalties, financial losses, and bankruptcy. It compares deceptive tactics in Nikola's hydrogen truck claims to Theranos' blood testing fraud, highlighting how overpromising without technical validation can undermine stakeholder trust and business sustainability.

## Key Points

- Nikola Electric Vehicles (2015) claimed a hydrogen truck prototype in 2018 that was later revealed as non-functional, leading to fraud convictions and bankruptcy.
- Trevor Milton, Nikola's CEO, was convicted of securities fraud, sentenced to four years in prison (2022), and pardoned in 2025 after $680 million in restitution.
- Nikola shipped 200 functional hydrogen trucks by 2024 but filed for bankruptcy due to overpromising, infrastructure gaps, and $1 billion annual losses from hydrogen operations.
- The 2018 prototype video showed a truck rolling under gravity, not self-propulsion, and was later deemed inaccurate by Nikola's 2020 annual report.
- Hydrogen prices rose from $15/kg in 2022 to $36/kg by 2024, far exceeding Nikola's claimed $3/kg target, highlighting supply chain and cost challenges.
- Nikola's bankruptcy in 2025 followed $384 million in 2020 losses and $1 billion annual losses after shifting to hydrogen trucks without adequate infrastructure.
- Elizabeth Holmes, Theranos founder, was pardoned by the DOJ after fraud convictions, released without civil penalties, and founded Cyberjet (AI aircraft company) despite ethical concerns.
- The lecture contrasts Nikola's deceptive marketing with Tesla's ethical success, stressing the need for transparent communication between vision ('what we want to do') and current functionality ('what we are doing').
- Investors must assess ethical red flags like transparency, accountability, and stakeholder alignment before investing, as highlighted by the organizational ethics audit assignment.
