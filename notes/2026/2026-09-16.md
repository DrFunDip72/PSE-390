## Summary

This study guide synthesizes ethical frameworks through real-world examples from Batman, Star Trek, and business scenarios. It contrasts deontology (rule-based ethics), utilitarianism (maximizing aggregate benefits), virtue ethics (moral character in action), and ethics of care (individual needs within relationships), highlighting their applications, pitfalls, and interplay in decision-making contexts.

## Key Points

- Deontology emphasizes duty, honor, and loyalty over utilitarian outcomes, as seen in Batman's refusal to kill the Joker due to trauma from his parents' deaths.
- Utilitarianism prioritizes maximizing net utility for the greatest number, exemplified by Star Trek's 'needs of the many' where Spock sacrifices himself to save the Enterprise.
- Ethics of care shifts focus from abstract rules to individual needs, illustrated by Professor Kahler's request for key card access to a faculty room for business purposes.
- Virtue ethics involves choosing right actions in critical moments, such as a CFP holder intervening in a crisis to balance courage and preparedness.
- Social contracts require reevaluating institutional rules to meet specific individual needs, like adjusting key card access for Professor Kahler's business requirements.
- Deontology's pitfall includes ignoring human rights violations for aggregate benefits, such as using marginalized groups as tools for greater outcomes.
- Ethics of care prioritizes treating individuals as real humans with specific needs over abstract categories like 'EMCOM faculty members' in institutional rules.
- The Savior exemplifies ethical integration by sacrificing for the many (demilitarianism), fulfilling justice laws (deontology), demonstrating courage (virtue ethics), and providing individual redemption (ethics of care).
