## Summary

The lecture explores ethical dilemmas in commodifying social values through real-world cases, emphasizing the risks of misaligned corporate values, addictive design practices, and the tension between profit and ethical responsibility. It uses examples like META's data privacy breaches, fake charities, and brand missteps with social movements to illustrate how commodifying values can lead to ethical conflicts, market backlash, and brand damage.

## Key Points

- Commodification of social values involves treating values or public goods as tradable goods, even when their intended value is non-monetary.
- META was ordered to pay $25 billion for privacy breaches, exemplifying commodification of data privacy.
- George Costanza's fake Human Fund charity highlighted ethical issues when charity value was sold without legitimacy.
- Three evaluation lenses for commodification: fairness (price access vs. need), corruption (price distorting meaning), and crowding out (money reducing motivation).
- Preschool fee example in Israel reduced parental punctuality due to perceived value of extra time, showing crowding out.
- Bud Light's transgender partnership caused boycotts, illustrating risks of misaligned political values in branding.
- Target's removal of Pride merchandise led to boycotts, emphasizing the need for value alignment with customer expectations.
- Ethical alignment requires understanding target demographics and avoiding abrupt value shifts that damage credibility.
- Addictive social media design raises ethical concerns about user choice, similar to tobacco industry practices.
- Instagram's impact on teen body image without adequate intervention parallels tobacco industry harm.
- Carbon offsetting claims must be transparent to avoid misleading consumers about environmental benefits.
- The Utah Attorney General's lawsuit against Meta highlights legal and ethical responsibilities in child data protection.
