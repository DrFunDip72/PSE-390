The lecture explores high trust culture through personal narratives, Michelangelo's David sculpture, and organizational examples, emphasizing the Sciences Center's mission to develop leaders who edify communities through character and capability. ## Key Points

- High trust culture: An environment where individuals feel confident in organizational integrity and reliability, enabling authentic self-expression and efficient collaboration.
- Edify: To build or strengthen through teaching and support (vs. literal 'edifice'), emphasizing leadership's role in nurturing communities.
- Michelangelo's David: Illustrates removing non-essential elements to reveal true form, analogous to becoming authentic in high trust environments.
- Christ-centered leadership: Relational model emphasizing love, agency, and accountability, where trust enables delegation without micromanaging.
- Organizational culture: Defined by shared norms, values, and unspoken assumptions that guide behavior, including artifacts and practices.
- Trust as interest on money: Those who earn trust through effective delegation save resources, while those lacking trust pay for mistakes or increased motivation costs.
- High trust vs. low trust: High trust accelerates outcomes with joy and efficiency; low trust causes gullibility, burnout, and unethical behavior due to over-monitoring.
- Cost of distrust: Includes lost efficiency, motivation costs, and ethical risks, as seen in examples like Elizabeth Holmes' case and roommate conflicts.
