## Summary

This lecture explores ethical decision-making in business through a case study involving liquidation of a bicycle business unit, emphasizing stakeholder theory and the tension between shareholder primacy and broader ethical responsibilities. Students analyze whether to inform a Brazilian supplier about impending cuts to protect 28 employees' jobs while balancing legal obligations (NDA) and ethical duties to the supplier. The discussion highlights historical shifts in corporate responsibility—from shareholder primacy (post-1970s) to the Business Roundtable's 2019 multi-stakeholder approach—illustrating how ethical frameworks like utilitarianism and virtue ethics guide decisions that impact employees, suppliers, and communities.

## Key Points

- Concept — Ethical conflict: Balancing legal obligations (NDA), CEO orders, personal ethics, and supplier employee risks in a liquidation scenario
- Concept — Stakeholder theory: Corporations historically prioritized shareholders (post-1970s) but now include all five stakeholders (employees, suppliers, customers, community, shareholders) per Business Roundtable (2019)
- Concept — Ethical frameworks: Utilitarianism (maximizing collective welfare) and virtue ethics (integrity) guide decisions on supplier communication during liquidation
- Concept — Case study: 4M bicycle business unit liquidation requires choosing between informing a Brazilian supplier to protect 28 employees or complying with CEO's order to prevent leaks
- Concept — Consequences of neglect: Apple's supplier bankruptcy, Marks & Spencer's supplier drop causing job losses, and Target's Canada exit damaging supplier relationships
- Concept — Proactive solutions: Toyota's Drive Transition Program reduces job losses through retraining and community funds, contrasting with short-term cuts that cause legal/reputational harm
- Concept — Decision criteria: Legal compliance, ethical impact on suppliers' communities, and long-term business sustainability over immediate cost savings
- Concept — Stakeholder priority shifts: From shareholder primacy (Milton Friedman) to multi-stakeholder approach (Business Roundtable) for sustainable value creation
- Concept — Ethical action: Informing suppliers with timelines to allow pivots while maintaining NDA integrity, avoiding harm to 28 employees
- Concept — Risk mitigation: Gradual order reductions, supplier buyouts, or legal exits to prevent leaks without compromising supplier communities
- Concept — Business Roundtable's role: 181 CEOs committed to stakeholder-inclusive practices for long-term resilience and shareholder value
- Concept — Ethical trade-offs: Short-term cost-cutting risks legal/ethical breaches, while stakeholder engagement ensures sustainable growth and community trust
- Concept — Real-world application: Toyota's transparent closure plan preserved jobs and reputation versus Apple's supplier bankruptcy from broken commitments
- Concept — Decision framework: Evaluate legal obligations, ethical duties, and community impact before acting on liquidation scenarios
- Big takeaway — This lecture explores ethical decision-making in business through a case study involving liquidation of a bicycle business unit, emphasizing stakeholder theory and the tension between shareholder primacy and broader ethical responsibilities.
