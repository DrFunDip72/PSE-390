## Part 1 · Six Steps to Sound Ethical Decision Making

### 1. Which sequence correctly lists the Six Steps to Sound Ethical Decision Making?

- **A.** Define the issues → Gather the facts → Check your gut → Identify stakeholders → Apply lenses → Option C
- **B.** Gather the facts → Define the ethical issues → Identify stakeholders → Identify obligations & consequences → Think creatively (Option C) → Check your gut
- **C.** Identify stakeholders → Gather the facts → Apply lenses → Define the issues → Check your gut → Option C
- **D.** Check your gut → Gather the facts → Define the issues → Option C → Identify stakeholders → Apply lenses

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. Facts first, gut check last. The order is Facts → Issues → Stakeholders → Obligations & Consequences (lenses) → Option C → Gut. Every other choice scrambles it.

*In class:* Sep 21: gather facts first, "jumping to a solution before the facts are known is often a first mistake." Define issues, then stakeholders, then the lenses, then a smarter "C," then check your gut.

</details>

### 2. Step 4 (of the Six Steps to Sound Ethical Decision Making) asks decision makers to identify obligations and consequences. What tool does the framework use for this step?

- **A.** A cost–benefit analysis
- **B.** A stakeholder map
- **C.** Ethical lenses (theoretical perspectives)
- **D.** A gut check

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C**. Step 4 = the lenses from the Ethics Toolkit (Deontology, Utilitarianism, Virtue Ethics, Social Contracts, Care). A stakeholder map is Step 3 and the gut check is Step 6.

</details>

### 3. A product team evaluating a new same-day delivery promise lists customers, shareholders, and the software engineers as affected parties. They do not consider the warehouse contract workers who will absorb the overtime. Which step was done incompletely?

- **A.** Step 1 — Gather the facts
- **B.** Step 2 — Define the ethical issues
- **C.** Step 3 — Identify the affected parties
- **D.** Step 5 — Think creatively about potential actions

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C**. Leaving out people who bear the cost is a stakeholder gap, which is Step 3. Notice the missing group is the one with the least voice, which is also what the Ethics of Care lens looks for.

*In class:* Sep 21: start with those immediately affected, then widen the circle to coworkers, communities, society, and even people not yet born. "Think generationally."

</details>

### 4. An analyst works through the first 5 Steps and every lens appears to support her plan, yet she feels uneasy about it. According to the framework, the best response is to:

- **A.** Ignore the feeling, because the analysis is complete
- **B.** Treat the unease as a signal to pause and re-examine the decision before acting
- **C.** Restart at Step 1 and discard the prior analysis
- **D.** Let a supervisor decide to help share responsibility

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. Step 6 (spider senses; the Spirit) treats unease as data. You do not override it (A), throw out good work (C), or hand off responsibility (D). You pause and re-examine.

*In class:* Sep 21: the gut is an early warning system, "spidey sense," and sometimes "the Spirit." Emotion comes first, reason steers, the gut confirms. "Always buy time," then check with a mentor and the social-media test.

</details>

### 5. A company finds a minor defect in a smart thermostat. Leadership frames the choice as "full recall" versus "say nothing." An engineer proposes a free software patch with a plain-language notice to every customer. The engineer's contribution best illustrates:

- **A.** Step 1 — Gather the facts
- **B.** Step 3 — Identify stakeholders
- **C.** Step 5 — Option C – Think creatively
- **D.** Step 6 — Check your gut

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C**. Two bad options framed as the only choices, then a third that serves everyone: that is Option C.

*In class:* Sep 21: "Don't force an A or B choice... so many times C is the smarter option." His example was Cummins, which funded a school for the kids stealing parts instead of prosecuting them or giving up.

</details>

## Part 2 · Defining Ethical Issues

### 6. You are a mid-level manager at a logistics firm. Your supervisor explicitly instructs you to falsify safety inspection logs to meet a strict shipping deadline, stating, "Everyone does it to keep things moving." According to the categories of defining ethical issues, what specific type of dilemma are you facing? *(judgment call)*

- **A.** Conflict of Interest
- **B.** Standing Up to Power
- **C.** Dissemblance
- **D.** Skirting the Rules

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. The deciding detail is that a person with authority over you is explicitly telling you to do wrong. That is Standing Up to Power. Skirting the Rules is when you are the one tempted to bend a rule. ("Everyone does it" is also a claim of normalcy rationalization.)

*In class:* Sep 14: his questions for this one are "do they have the right to do this?" and "would others agree with me?" Example: the Wells Fargo tellers who stood up.

</details>

### 7. Which of the following best describes the "Dissemblance" category of ethical issues?

- **A.** Blindly obeying a corrupt authority figure
- **B.** Concealing or shading the truth because you believe it will produce a better outcome than full disclosure
- **C.** Using unfair means because everyone else is doing it
- **D.** Forgiving a mistake that is yours to enforce

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. Dissemblance is hiding or softening the truth for a supposedly better result. A is Standing Up to Power, C is Playing Dirty, D is Showing Mercy.

</details>

### 8. Three months ago you agreed to be a groomsman in a friend's wedding. Last week you were offered a once-in-a-career job interview that falls on the wedding day and cannot be rescheduled.

- **A.** Made a Promise and the World Has Changed
- **B.** Sacrificing Personal Values or Relationships
- **C.** Unfair Advantage
- **D.** Showing Mercy

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A**. A prior commitment plus new circumstances that make it costly. The handout asks: can I still keep it even at cost, and does keeping it make other promises harder?

*In class:* Sep 14: Boeing 737 Max was his example of a promise where the world changed. Sep 23 (Wired for Hire): you made an offer, the situation changed, do you renege? "Sometimes it's a yes and sometimes it's a no."

</details>

### 9. You sit on a scholarship selection committee. Your younger cousin is one of the finalists. *(judgment call)*

- **A.** Loyalty
- **B.** Unfair Advantage
- **C.** Conflict of Interest
- **D.** Suspicions Without Enough Evidence

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C**. Personal stake versus a duty to be objective. Loyalty also appears, but the decision-making role is what makes this a conflict of interest. Handout: who has a right to know, and have I told them?

*In class:* Sep 14: McKinsey advised both the OxyContin maker and the FDA, which resulted in billions in fines.

</details>

### 10. As a teaching assistant, you enforce a strict late-work penalty. A student who missed the deadline because of a family emergency asks you to waive it.

- **A.** Loyalty
- **B.** Showing Mercy
- **C.** Conflict of Interest
- **D.** Standing Up to Power

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. Someone asks you to ease a consequence that is yours to enforce. Handout: is mercy mine to give? Who bears the cost?

*In class:* Sep 14: his example was a Cisco intruder: internal reprimand, or turn him in as an example. No conclusion was reached.

</details>

### 11. Your mentor, who helped you secure your dream job, asks you to quietly route a minor client invoice through an auxiliary account to avoid a local tax bracket. You owe your career to this person. Which category of ethical issues is most prominent here?

- **A.** Repair
- **B.** Loyalty
- **C.** Unfair Advantage
- **D.** Showing Mercy

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. "You owe your career to this person" is the loyalty cue. Handout: have they shown loyalty to me, and would they want it if they knew all the costs?

*In class:* Sep 14: Boeing employees felt they had to be loyal instead of blowing the whistle. Sep 30: loyalty is an important virtue but creates pressure to conform, and "can blind you."

</details>

### 12. A rival firm has been spreading false rumors about your company to win clients. A colleague suggests anonymously leaking the rival's internal pricing sheet, which he obtained from a former employee, "to level the playing field."

- **A.** Intervention
- **B.** Repair
- **C.** Playing Dirty
- **D.** Made a Promise and the World Has Changed

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C**. Using unethical means to get a fair outcome. Handout: would this really bring justice, and does harming the other party help anyone besides you?

*In class:* Sep 14: HP used false identities to get directors' and journalists' phone records while hunting a board leak. He ranked Skirting the Rules as "slightly less bad" than Playing Dirty.

</details>

### 13. You notice a coworker consistently taking home expensive office technology under the radar. You don't have hard proof yet, only missing inventory logs and suspicious late-night office entries. What type of ethical issue does this situation represent?

- **A.** Intervention
- **B.** Suspicions Without Enough Evidence
- **C.** Playing Dirty
- **D.** Loyalty

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. The key phrase is "don't have hard proof." Handout: what happens if I act and the allegation is false?

</details>

### 14. (Select all that apply.) Your best friend is on your project team and has been underperforming. Your manager asks you for a candid peer evaluation that will affect your friend's raise. You are tempted to soften the truth. Which ethical issues are present? *(judgment call)*

- **A.** Loyalty
- **B.** Dissemblance
- **C.** Sacrificing Personal Values or Relationships
- **D.** Unfair Advantage

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A, B, C**. A (friend) and B (tempted to soften the truth) are clear. C is the judgment call: being honest puts a real cost on your friend and the friendship. D does not fit; nobody holds an unearned edge.

</details>

## Part 3 · The Ethics Toolkit

### 15. The question "What if everyone did it?" tests which decision criterion, from which theory?

- **A.** Utility — Utilitarianism
- **B.** Universality — Deontology
- **C.** Disclosure — Virtue Ethics
- **D.** Social obligations — Social Contracts

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. Universality, from Kant's Deontology.

*In class:* Sep 16: the universal law test is "if I do this, is it OK for everyone else to do it? If not, it fails." Example: pinning a mistake on an intern who already quit fails it.

</details>

### 16. Under Utilitarianism (John Stuart Mill), an action is ethical when it:

- **A.** Treats people as ends in themselves
- **B.** Is consistent with who I want to become
- **C.** Maximizes happiness and minimizes pain, providing the greatest benefit to the most people
- **D.** Meets the needs of the most vulnerable

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C**. A is Deontology (dignity), B is Virtue Ethics (integrity), D is Ethics of Care.

*In class:* Sep 16: Utilitarianism judges by outcomes, the greatest net utility for the greatest number. His example was Spock: "the needs of the many outweigh the needs of the few." Pitfall: who bears the negative externalities?

</details>

### 17. Aristotle's Virtue Ethics emphasizes personal character and self-reflection. Which of the following questions reflects this philosophical approach?

- **A.** "What kind of person would this action make me? What would the best person in the world do?"
- **B.** "What if everyone adopted this behavior as a universal law?"
- **C.** "Who is most vulnerable in this situation?"
- **D.** "Am I fulfilling my explicit contractual duties to the state?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A**. B is Deontology, C is Care, D is Social Contracts.

*In class:* Sep 16: virtue ethics is character and habit, the mean between excess and deficiency (courage sits between paralyzing fear and recklessness). "We are what we repeatedly do."

</details>

### 18. Which pair of questions best represents the Ethics of Care?

- **A.** "What if everyone did it?" and "What obligations do I have?"
- **B.** "Who is vulnerable here?" and "Who has put their trust in me?"
- **C.** "Would I want this broadcast?" and "Can I live with my decision?"
- **D.** "Am I fulfilling my societal duties?" and "What kind of world do I want to live in?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. Care has two criteria: care (the vulnerable) and relationships (those who trust you). A is Deontology, C is Virtue (disclosure), D is Social Contracts.

*In class:* Sep 16: care is relational and context-bound, rooted in vulnerability and interdependence. Example: a no-exceptions PTO policy versus an analyst whose father is dying. He called it virtue ethics plus "seeing people as people."

</details>

### 19. A regional hospital system plans to close a free clinic in a low-income neighborhood. The savings would fund upgrades that benefit far more patients system-wide. Which pairing best describes how two lenses would view the closure?

- **A.** Utilitarianism tends to support it; Ethics of Care tends to oppose it
- **B.** Utilitarianism tends to oppose it; Ethics of Care tends to support it
- **C.** Both lenses clearly support it
- **D.** Neither lens is relevant to the decision

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A**. Utilitarianism counts the greater benefit to more patients. Care focuses on the vulnerable neighborhood losing its only access.

*In class:* Sep 16: his pitfall for utilitarianism is hiding harm to a few behind an acceptable total. His care example gave the last implementation slot to the smaller firm that would fail without it, "even if you lose" the large client.

</details>

### 20. A CEO considering an aggressive tax-avoidance scheme asks his team, "How would this look on the front page of the newspaper?" He is applying:

- **A.** Justice (Deontology)
- **B.** Universality (Deontology)
- **C.** Disclosure (Virtue Ethics)
- **D.** Relationships (Ethics of Care)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C**. "Broadcast to the world" and "front page" are the Disclosure rule, which sits under Virtue Ethics.

*In class:* Sep 21: "the disclosure rule." Would you be fine reading about it on social media? He also quoted Jefferson on never harboring a thought you would not avow openly.

</details>

### 21. Social Contract theory (associated with Locke, Rousseau, and Rawls) evaluates ethical decisions based on whether:

- **A.** You are fulfilling your obligation to contribute to a harmonious society and respecting societal duties
- **B.** The action maximizes short-term shareholder value
- **C.** The decision feels true to your inner personal virtues
- **D.** The action treats people strictly as a means to an end

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A**. Social obligations. B is not a lens, C is Virtue, D is the opposite of Deontology's dignity criterion.

*In class:* Sep 16: social contracts are implicit mutual agreements between an organization and society. Example: changing commissions mid-year, where "the fight is not about the math, it's about the bargain."

</details>

### 22. A software engineer finds a critical security flaw in her company's new banking app. Her boss tells her to bury the report because patching it will delay the product launch and crash the stock price. Which ethical lenses most directly support the engineer's duty to blow the whistle or refuse the order?

- **A.** Utilitarianism exclusively, because short-term stock crashes matter more than long-term trust
- **B.** Ethics of Care, because software code has feelings
- **C.** Social Contract theory, because tax brackets are shifting
- **D.** Deontology (protecting user rights and dignity rather than using them as a means to profit) and the Disclosure Rule

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D**. Users would be used as a means to profit (dignity), and burying the flaw would not survive being broadcast (Disclosure). The other options are distractors.

*In class:* Sep 16: his deontology examples were "do not lie" and "do not use people as tools," including refusing a CFO's request to backdate a disclosure even though it would change only the optics.

</details>

## Part 4 · Rationalization

### 23. What is the primary definition of moral rationalization?

- **A.** Making ethical decisions based strictly on company policy
- **B.** Convincing oneself that unethical behavior somehow does not violate ethical standards
- **C.** Accepting full responsibility for a mistake
- **D.** Seeking guidance from a higher moral authority

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. Straight from the handout: convincing ourselves that unethical behavior does not violate our standards.

</details>

### 24. A sales rep is caught backdating a client contract. She responds, "Talk to my manager — she's the one who told me to do it." Which rationalization is she using?

- **A.** Denial of responsibility
- **B.** Claim of normalcy
- **C.** Denial of victim
- **D.** Euphemistic language

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A**. Shifting blame to another party.

*In class:* Oct 5: denial of responsibility, "probably one of the most common." Volkswagen blamed a couple of engineers, who said they were "just following orders."

</details>

### 25. An intern takes several boxes of office supplies home and tells himself, "The company buys this stuff by the pallet. Nobody is going to miss a few pens." This is:

- **A.** Moral ledger
- **B.** Advantageous comparison
- **C.** Denial of injury
- **D.** Condemnation of condemners

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C**. "Nobody is going to miss it" argues that no one was hurt.

*In class:* Oct 5: denial of injury. Shkreli's lawyers argued "no harm, no foul" because investors made money. His rebuttal: a good outcome does not erase a dishonest act.

</details>

### 26. A restaurant owner is cited for not paying overtime. He tells the labor inspector, "You people just need to hit your quota of fines. You've never run a business a day in your life." This is an example of:

- **A.** Condemnation of condemners
- **B.** Denial of victim
- **C.** Claim of normalcy
- **D.** Appeal to higher loyalty

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A**. He attacks the person judging him instead of addressing the violation.

*In class:* Oct 5: condemnation of the condemners, going after critics or whistleblowers. Example: Wirecard attacking journalists. "You just dig a deeper hole."

</details>

### 27. A startup founder reasons, "We give 10% of our profits to clean-water charities, so inflating our user numbers a bit in the investor deck isn't a big deal." Which rationalization is this?

- **A.** Denial of negative intent
- **B.** Advantageous comparison
- **C.** Appeal to higher loyalty
- **D.** Moral ledger

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D**. Good deeds in one place are used to offset bad behavior in another.

*In class:* Oct 5: moral ledger. Examples were Lance Armstrong's cancer foundation, and the reverse form, "I'm underpaid so fudging is fine." "Ethics is not a bank account."

</details>

### 28. An accountant who "smooths" quarterly earnings tells a colleague, "Compared to what happened at Enron, adjusting a few accruals is nothing." Which rationalization is she using?

- **A.** Claim of normalcy
- **B.** Advantageous comparison
- **C.** Moral ledger
- **D.** Denial of responsibility

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. She points to an extreme case so hers looks mild. Normalcy would be "everyone smooths earnings."

*In class:* Oct 5: advantageous comparison. Shkreli said his drug was cheap next to $150k-a-year cancer drugs, and an investment firm said "at least we're not Madoff."

</details>

### 29. (Select all that apply.) A small-business owner mislabels imported goods to avoid tariffs. He says: "If I paid the full tariff, I'd have to lay off my whole staff — I had no choice. I wasn't trying to cheat anyone; my heart was in the right place. In our books, it's just listed as 'tariff optimization.'" Which rationalizations appear?

- **A.** Defense of necessity
- **B.** Denial of negative intent
- **C.** Denial of victim
- **D.** Euphemistic language

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A, B, D**. "I had no choice" = necessity. "My heart was in the right place" = negative intent. "Tariff optimization" = euphemism. He never blames a victim, so C is out.

*In class:* Oct 5: necessity ("I had no choice") often shows up under the utilitarian lens. Euphemisms: VW's "acoustic function," "workforce optimization," "right-sizing." Intent matters but "doesn't undo the impact."

</details>

## Part 5 · Integrated

### 30. You are a new analyst. In the hallway, you watch a senior manager berate and belittle a summer intern in front of others. No one says anything. Which combination of ethical issue and lens best fits this situation?

- **A.** Repair — Utilitarianism
- **B.** Intervention — Ethics of Care (care for the most vulnerable)
- **C.** Conflict of Interest — Social Contracts
- **D.** Unfair Advantage — Virtue Ethics

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B**. You see wrongdoing and must decide whether to step in, which is Intervention. The intern is the vulnerable person without a voice, so the Care lens applies.

*In class:* Sep 14: he equated Intervention with whistleblowing (example: Susan Fowler at Uber).

</details>

### 31. A nonprofit director runs an after-school program. Her grant prohibits spending on food, but many children arrive hungry. She is tempted to reclassify snack purchases as "educational supplies." Which response best reflects Step 5 (Option C) for this Skirting the Rules dilemma?

- **A.** Reclassify the purchases; the goal is worthwhile
- **B.** Follow the rule and do nothing about the hunger
- **C.** Ask the funder for a budget modification and partner with a local food bank to supply snacks
- **D.** Resign, hoping it sends a statement that influences change

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C**. Option C finds a third path that honors the rule and meets the need. A breaks the rule, B ignores the need, D abandons the kids.

*In class:* Sep 21: Option C in practice. In the plant-closure case it was to discourage the neighbor's purchase without revealing why and to push for an earlier announcement.

</details>

### 32. (Select all that apply.) An older teammate hands a student-athlete a copy of next week's exam, which the student never asked for. The student recognizes this as an Unfair Advantage. Which Toolkit criteria would counsel against using the exam? *(judgment call)*

- **A.** Universality — "What if everyone did it?"
- **B.** Disclosure — "Would I want this broadcast to the world?"
- **C.** Integrity — "What action best reflects the kind of person I want to be?"
- **D.** None — because he didn't ask for the exam, no ethical issue exists

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A, B, C**. Using the exam fails universality, disclosure and integrity. D is wrong: receiving an unfair edge without asking does not make it fair. Justice and agency also apply, but they were not offered.

</details>

### 33. A sales manager's client asks whether an order will ship by Friday. The manager knows there is roughly a 50% chance of delay. Saying a confident "yes" will likely close the deal. This is a Dissemblance dilemma. Which response best honors the agency criterion?

- **A.** Say "yes" and hope it ships on time
- **B.** Say "yes" and offer a discount if it's late
- **C.** Explain the real risk of delay so the client can plan and decide for themselves
- **D.** Avoid answering and change the subject

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C**. Agency means letting others make their own choices, which requires true information. A, B and D all deprive the client of it.

*In class:* Sep 21: in the customer-health-score case he argued for honesty and flagging the risk early, because the team is responsible for the client's outcome.

</details>
